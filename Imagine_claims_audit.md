"Apenas se movió" is defensible as a decade-scale statement but is a characterization, not a single statistic. Keep, but be ready to cite INE household surveys (EPHPM) and World Bank poverty data if challenged.
Source: [World Bank — Honduras](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/honduras/overview); INE Honduras EPHPM.

## Chapter IV — La paz / Peace (was III)

**Claim 18. Mitch, Eta, Iota as defining disaster-response moments**
✓ Verified. Hurricane Mitch (1998), Eta and Iota (November 2020) are the reference catastrophes of modern Honduran history; military disaster relief was central in all three. Uncontroversial.

**Claim 18b. "Honduras es, año tras año, uno de los países más golpeados del mundo por el clima extremo"**
✓ Verified. Germanwatch's Global Climate Risk Index ranked Honduras the single most affected country in the world over 1998–2017, and it remains in the top group of the long-term index. Widely cited by UN agencies and the World Bank.
Source: [Germanwatch — Global Climate Risk Index](https://www.germanwatch.org/en/cri)

**Claim 18c. "vienen más huracanes, y más fuertes"**
✓ Supported by scientific consensus, with a precision note. IPCC AR6 projects an increasing *proportion* of intense (Category 4–5) tropical cyclones and higher rainfall rates as the climate warms; total hurricane *frequency* may not rise. "Más fuertes" is solidly supported; "más" (more numerous) is the softer half of the claim. In a manifesto register this is acceptable; the strictest wording would be "huracanes más fuertes y más destructivos."
Source: [IPCC AR6 WGI — tropical cyclone projections](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/)

**Claim 18d. "un país pequeño que casi no contamina puede ser de los primeros en alcanzar emisiones netas cero"**
✓ Verified premise. Honduras emits roughly 0.02–0.06% of global greenhouse gases (~10–20 MtCO₂e/yr depending on inventory scope) — negligible at world scale. The net-zero attainability is an aspiration, not a claim of fact; the premise ("casi no contamina") is sound.
Source: [Climate Watch — Honduras GHG emissions](https://www.climatewatchdata.org/countries/HND)

**Claim 19. "Tenemos vecinos muy cercanos que ya han logrado prosperar sin ejército"**
✓ Verified. Costa Rica abolished its army in 1948 (constitutional Art. 12); Panama abolished its military in 1990 (constitutionalized 1994). Both outperform the regional average on human development.
Sources: [Costa Rica — Constitution Art. 12 / abolition of the army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Costa_Rica) · [Panama — abolition of military, 1990/1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamanian_Public_Forces)

## Chapter XV — La firma / The signature (was XIV)

**Claim 28. "La independencia se proclamó un 15 de septiembre"**
✓ Verified. September 15, 1821.


**Claim 29. "La ley hondureña permite candidaturas independientes… con las firmas suficientes de su departamento"**
✓ Verified. The Electoral Law (Decreto 35-2021) regulates independent candidacies for president, Congress, and mayorships; congressional candidates require signatures equal to 2% of departmental votes, plus registration formalities.
Sources: [Ley Electoral de Honduras – Decreto 35-2021](https://aceproject.org/ero-en/regions/americas/HN/honduras-ley-electoral-2021-con-reformas-2021/) · [Proceso — candidaturas independientes](https://proceso.hn/aprueban-las-candidaturas-independientes-en-la-nueva-ley-electoral-de-honduras/)



**Claim 30. "Honduras abolió el reclutamiento forzoso en 1994"**
✓ Verified. Constitutional reform (Decreto 24-94) amended Article 276; military service is voluntary in peacetime. The change followed years of denunciations of forced recruitment raids ("redadas"/"cacerías") by human rights organizations. Note: proposals to reinstate obligatory service were presented in Congress in 2026 — the manifesto's voluntary framing is deliberately on the opposite side of that debate.
Sources: [Decreto 24-94 — reforma art. 276](https://www.poderjudicial.gob.hn/Cedij/CR/A%C3%91O%201994/Decreto%20Numero%2024-94%20reforma%20art%20276%20constitucion.pdf) · [La Prensa — derogación del servicio militar obligatorio](https://www.laprensa.hn/honduras/la-derogacion-del-servicio-militar-obligatorio-fue-un-error-historico-DWLP1005237) · [Criterio.hn — proyecto para su retorno](https://criterio.hn/diputado-de-alianza-patriotica-presenta-proyecto-de-ley-para-el-retorno-del-servicio-militar-obligatorio-en-honduras/)



**Claim 31. "La Corte Suprema y el Ministerio Público se elegirán por concurso público, con audiencias transmitidas y veeduría ciudadana"**
Policy proposal, not a factual claim. Context: Honduran Supreme Court magistrates are elected by Congress from a list prepared by a nominating board, a process repeatedly criticized for party negotiation. No verification needed; defensible as reform.

**Claim 32. Police welfare commitments (health coverage, survivor's pension)**
Policy proposal. Costed within the L7,100M/yr salary estimate only for salaries; health coverage and pensions would be additional and are not quantified in the text. If challenged on cost, the honest answer is that the figure cited covers salaries alone.



**Claim 33. "En el Reino Unido, en Noruega, en Irlanda y en Nueva Zelanda la policía ordinaria patrulla desarmada"**
✓ Verified. In all four countries the majority of officers do not routinely carry firearms; armed response is provided by specialized units (UK: Authorised Firearms Officers; Norway: firearms stored in vehicles, authorization required; Ireland: Garda ERU/ASU; New Zealand: Armed Offenders Squad). Norway is the closest partial exception (weapons carried in locked vehicle safes since 2018) — the manifesto's phrasing "patrulla desarmada" remains accurate for all four.
Sources: [UK Home Office — police use of firearms statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/police-use-of-firearms-statistics) · [An Garda Síochána](https://www.garda.ie) · [New Zealand Police — firearms](https://www.police.govt.nz)

**Claim 34. "Costa Rica no tiene ejército desde 1948 y Panamá desde 1990"**
✓ Verified. Costa Rica abolished its army by the 1949 Constitution (Art. 12), following the 1948 civil war — the December 1948 abolition date is standard. Panama abolished its military in 1990 after the US invasion, constitutionalized in 1994. Note the careful wording "a ninguno de los dos le ha ido peor por eso" — it avoids the claim that neither has been invaded, which would be false for Panama (1989, before abolition).
Sources: [Costa Rica Constitution, Art. 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Costa_Rica) · [Panamanian Public Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamanian_Public_Forces)

**Claim 35. Funding sequence ("la partida secreta paga lo inmediato; la reforma tributaria del año tres paga lo universal")**
✓ Internally coherent. This paragraph exists to resolve an arithmetic objection: the identified immediate source (L12,171M/yr secret account) cannot fund universal health and school reform. The text now assigns immediate commitments to the account and privileges, universal programs to the year-three tax reform (L77,483M/yr in exemptions is the relevant pool), and names some goals as horizon rather than first-term promises. No figure is claimed for universal health — deliberately, since a credible estimate would require actuarial work.

**Claim 36. El Salvador comparison (chapter XI)**
Characterization, not a statistic. The text avoids citing homicide figures or prison population, and makes no factual claim beyond "resultados rápidos" — which is uncontested. Deliberate: any specific number about El Salvador would invite a debate the manifesto does not need.



**Claim 37. "Australia… más de 650,000; Reino Unido 1997; Nueva Zelanda 2019… más de 56,000"**
✓ Verified. Australia's 1996 National Firearms Agreement, after the Port Arthur massacre, banned semi-automatic and pump-action long guns and bought back more than 650,000 weapons (~US$304M program cost, 1996–97). The UK's Firearms (Amendment) (No. 2) Act 1997 banned civilian handgun ownership almost entirely, producing the largest firearms surrender in British history, completed without serious incident. New Zealand's post-Christchurch buyback collected about 56,250 firearms from ~33,000 people over six months in 2019.
Note: all three banned *categories* of weapons, not all lethal weapons. The manifesto (ch. V) was revised to match this precedent: it bans handguns, assault rifles and automatic/semi-automatic weapons for civilians, while hunting and rural working firearms remain legal under strict licensing. The comparison is now precedent-aligned in both scope and mechanism.
Sources: [Harvard — Australian gun buyback](https://content.sph.harvard.edu/wwwhsph/sites/1264/2012/10/bulletins_australia_spring_2011.pdf) · [Australian Parliament — National Firearms Agreement](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/history-milestones/australian-parliament-history-timeline/events/national-firearms-agreement) · [Firearms (Amendment) (No. 2) Act 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/64/enacted) · [CNN — New Zealand collects ~56,000 guns](https://www.cnn.com/2019/12/21/asia/new-zealand-gun-buyback-intl)



**Claim 38. "Las armas de guerra ya están prohibidas para los civiles en Honduras… y aun así circulan"**
✓ Verified. The Ley de Control de Armas de Fuego, Municiones, Explosivos y Materiales Relacionados prohibits for civilians all automatic firearms, silenced and high-precision weapons, and specifically the AK-47 (all versions), AR-15, M16 and M4 in 7.62×39 and 5.56/.223 NATO — reserved to the Armed Forces and National Police. What civilians may legally register: revolvers (.22–.44 Magnum), semi-automatic pistols (.22, .25, .380, 9mm, .40, .45), shotguns (12/16/20/28 gauge) and rifles up to .223, with a maximum of five registered weapons per person (reform proposals would lower this to three). The manifesto's ban therefore adds handguns to an existing prohibition, and the "circulan de todos modos" claim is well documented in Honduran crime reporting.
Sources: [Ley de Control de Armas de Fuego (TSC)](https://www.tsc.gob.hn/web/leyes/LEY%20DE%20CONTROL%20DE%20ARMAS%20DE%20FUEGO,%20MUNICIONES,%20EXPLOSIONES%20Y%20OTROS%20SIMILARES.pdf) · [UNAH — análisis de la ley](https://investigacionjuridica.unah.edu.hn/assets/Investigacion-Juridica/paginas/boletin-informativo-2019/Ley-de-Control-de-Armas-de-Fuego-Municiones-Explosivos-y-Materiales-Relacionados.pdf) · [La Prensa — nueva ley de portación](https://www.laprensa.hn/honduras/nueva-ley-armas-uso-permiso-portacion-honduras-MCLP1258477)

⚠️ **Verify before print:** "las armas con las que más se mata en Honduras" — firearms account for the large majority of Honduran homicides, but a type-level breakdown (handgun vs. long gun) should be confirmed with Observatorio de la Violencia (UNAH-IUDPAS) data before publication.

## PART II — Proposals checked against existing Honduran law
*Purpose: ensure the manifesto never proposes as new something already on the books. Where a law exists, the text was revised to demand enforcement rather than creation — which is both accurate and rhetorically stronger.*

**A. Forfeiture of illicit assets — ALREADY EXISTS.** The *Ley sobre Privación Definitiva del Dominio de Bienes de Origen Ilícito* is in force, and the Unidad de Información Financiera operates under the CNBS. Forfeiture is an autonomous action, independent of criminal conviction.
→ Ch. XI revised: "no hace falta inventar la herramienta… lo que falta es usarlas."
Sources: [Ley de Privación Definitiva del Dominio (TSC)](https://www.tsc.gob.hn/biblioteca/index.php/leyes/145-ley-sobre-privacion-definitiva-del-dominio-de-bienes-de-origen-ilicito) · [Ministerio Público — procedimiento de extinción de dominio](https://www.mp.hn/publicaciones/como-entender-el-procedimiento-de-extincion-o-privacion-de-dominio-de-bienes/)

**B. School meals — ALREADY EXIST.** The Programa Nacional de Alimentación Escolar (PNAE) reaches ~1.2–1.33 million children in ~22,000 schools, buys from small local producers, and covers up to 35% of daily nutritional requirements. It is sustained by annual agreements (WFP/PMA), with an investment above L840M for 2026.
→ Ch. IX revised: the manifesto no longer "creates" school meals; it makes them a permanent state obligation, upgrades snack to hot lunch, and removes donor dependence.
Sources: [SE — PNAE](https://www.se.gob.hn/inicio-sdgpsbe-perfil-programa-alimentacion/) · [Proceso — acuerdo PMA 2026](https://proceso.hn/honduras-y-pma-firman-acuerdo-que-garantiza-alimentacion-escolar-de-1-2-millones-de-ninos-durante-todo-2026/)

**C. Full-day schooling — ALREADY ANNOUNCED.** The government has announced *jornada extendida* (7:00–15:00) beginning 2027, adding lunch for full-day students.
→ Ch. IX revised: the manifesto now commits to making it real and permanent rather than claiming the idea.
Sources: [El Heraldo — tres horas más de clases desde 2027](https://www.elheraldo.hn/honduras/centros-educativos-impartiran-tres-horas-mas-clases-partir-del-2027-CG30416150) · [Hondudiario — objetivos de la jornada extendida](https://www.hondudiario.com/2026/05/10/con-jornada-extendida-se-quiere-subir-rendimiento-y-reducir-hambre-y-violencia-segun-educacion/)

**D. Solid waste — LAW EXISTS, UNENFORCED.** The *Ley para la Gestión Integral de Residuos Sólidos* (Decreto 57-2020) mandates reduction, reuse, recycling and the conversion of open dumps to sanitary landfills. Reported failures: weak application, limited inspection, insufficient investment.
→ Ch. VII revised: "La ley que ordena cerrarlos existe desde 2020: lo que nunca llegó fue el presupuesto ni la voluntad."
Source: [MiAmbiente — gestión integral de residuos sólidos](http://www.miambiente.gob.hn/blog/view/miambiente-presento-iniciativa-de-ley-para-gestion-integral-de-residuos-solidos)

**E. Asset declarations — MANDATORY BUT SECRET.** Officials must file annual sworn declarations of income and assets, but their publication is legally prohibited, and the TSC has lacked effective verification.
→ Ch. XII revised to name this precisely: declarations "hoy obligatorias pero secretas" will be made public. This is a genuine legal change, not a restatement.
Sources: [IUDPAS/UNAH — La transparencia en Honduras](https://iudpas.unah.edu.hn/dmsdocument/10541-la-transparencia-en-honduras) · [Ley de Transparencia y Acceso a la Información Pública](https://www.tsc.gob.hn/biblioteca/index.php/leyes/17-ley-de-transparencia-y-acceso-a-la-informacion-publica)

**F. Open contracting — PARTIALLY EXISTS.** ONCAE/Honducompras publication of procedures and contracts is already legally mandatory. The manifesto's contribution is completeness, machine-readable data and enforcement, not the principle.
→ No text change required; wording ("contratación abierta") is compatible. If challenged, the honest answer is: the portal exists, the compliance does not.

**G. Renewable energy — FRAMEWORK EXISTS.** The *Ley de Promoción a la Generación de Energía Eléctrica con Recursos Renovables* is in force; CREE is regulating distributed generation, storage and green hydrogen for 2026, with ~L12,578M (US$510M) public investment announced in the sector.
→ No change needed: the manifesto proposes a *household roof industry* (solar + rainwater harvesting), which is not covered by existing programs. Note for defense: the novelty is the housing/manufacturing angle, not renewables policy.
Sources: [Ley de Promoción de Energía Renovable (TSC)](https://www.tsc.gob.hn/biblioteca/index.php/leyes/65-ley-de-promocion-a-la-generacion-de-energia-electrica-con-recursos-renovables) · [Energía Estratégica — inversión 2026](https://www.energiaestrategica.com/honduras-inyecta-usd-510-millones-para-transformar-su-matriz-energetica-en-2026/)

**H. Firearms — WAR WEAPONS ALREADY BANNED.** See Claim 38. Ch. V revised accordingly; the genuinely new proposal is the handgun sale ban plus a paid buyback.

**I. Conscription — ALREADY VOLUNTARY.** See Claim 30. Ch. IV revised to defend the 1994 reform rather than appear to reverse it.

**J. Judicial selection — EXISTING MECHANISM.** Supreme Court magistrates are elected by Congress from a list prepared by a Junta Nominadora; the Fiscal General likewise from a proposing board. The manifesto's proposal (public competition, live national broadcast, no party negotiation) is a genuine change to how that mechanism operates.
→ No text change; defensible as reform.


**K. Voting from abroad — PARTIALLY EXISTS.** Hondurans abroad may vote, but **only for president and presidential designates**. The 2025 registry counted ~399,000 voters in the United States (~6% of the 6.5M national registry), tripling 2021.
→ Ch. XIII revised: the manifesto no longer implies the diaspora has no vote; it asks for the specific extension — congressional and municipal ballots.
Sources: [Proceso — voto de hondureños en el extranjero](https://proceso.hn/voto-de-hondurenos-en-el-extranjero-una-minoria-en-crecimiento/) · [Ley Electoral 2021](https://pdba.georgetown.edu/Electoral/Honduras/Leyes/LeyeElectoral.pdf)

**L. Deuda política — PAID TO PARTIES, NOT INDEPENDENTS.** The CNE pays L20 per valid vote to each political *party* (with a floor of 15% of the top party's allocation). It is not established that independent candidacies receive it.
→ Ch. XII revised to conditional: "todo financiamiento público que pudiera corresponderle… cada lempira que llegara a sus manos." Avoids promising to give away money the movement might never legally receive.

⚠️ **Sobering context for the signature strategy (ch. XV).** In 2025 an independent presidential candidacy required **67,161 signatures** (2% of valid votes in the previous general election). The independent movement *Nueva Generación* was rejected by the CNE for presenting only 1,323. The manifesto's call for independents in all eighteen departments is legally accurate but operationally very demanding — worth knowing before anyone quotes it as easy.
Source: [La Prensa — revisión de 31 candidaturas independientes](https://www.laprensa.hn/honduras/en-el-cne-concluyen-revision-de-31-candidaturas-independientes-ME26447847)


### Remaining novelties (nothing equivalent in Honduran law)
Handgun sale ban with paid buyback · melting state weapons into public works · unarmed ordinary policing · conversion of the army into a nature-and-rescue institution · elimination of the presidential confidential account · voluntary paid civic service · happiness as an official national metric · the leadership covenant (capped wealth, single term, cabinet named before the election, renunciation of public campaign financing).
